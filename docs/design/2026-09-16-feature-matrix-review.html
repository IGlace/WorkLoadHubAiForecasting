The feature matrix, explained

What the forecast reads from the database, what it computes from those numbers, what the model actually sees, and where a rule was invented rather than found. Written for the review before the switch from absences to personal_leaves.

1 · How a forecast is made

The model predicts one number: the hours a member will log in a future week. Everything else is either an input to that prediction or arithmetic done after it. The language model never touches a number; it reads the finished facts and writes prose.

The four ideas that make it work

  1. The target is logged hours per member-week. The truth the model is trained on is the sum of time_logs.hours for a member in a Monday-to-Sunday week. It is what the accuracy check scores too, so training and scoring never disagree.
  2. Every row is a week in a member's past, with the future hidden. A row for week w only contains what was knowable at the end of w. Its targets are the hours logged in w+1, w+2, … A booster per horizon learns "given how the last weeks looked, how many hours came h weeks later".
  3. The backtest is the honesty check. Six times, the run pretends it is a past Monday, fits only on rows old enough not to overlap the test week, predicts, and compares with what really got logged. The errors give the mean absolute error and the 10th and 90th percentile offsets that become the band around each forecast.
  4. A week is then placed on days. The predicted week is spread over the weekdays the member actually logs on, in the proportions of their last 13 weeks, skipping holidays and full leave days. Each day's demand is compared with that day's capacity. Overload is the excess and is never capped.

5 · Invented rules and assumptions

You asked which features were invented rather than read. No feature column is fabricated out of nothing: every one traces back to real table columns. But nine rules on the way are the module's own, not the application's, and two columns the application does record are deliberately not used. Each is a question for you.

A · The assignment date does not exist in the database

WorkloadHub stores who a task is assigned to, not when. The module reads the latest task_history row that set the assignee to the current one and takes its changed_at; when no such row exists it falls back to created_date and reports the task as an unresolved assignment in the data-quality facts. Feeds arrivals_13w, all the share columns, the arrival series, cycle_days_13w, open_* and the team backlog.

Question: does the application write an assignee row into task_history on every reassignment, including the first one at creation?

B · "Fresh" arrivals: the two-day rule

A task is fresh when it was assigned less than two days after being created; otherwise it is treated as pulled from a backlog. Only fresh tasks count in arrival_hrs_lag1..4 and weeks_since_last_arrival. The threshold is a constant (Lifecycle.BACKLOG_LAG_DAYS = 2) chosen for the generated data, never validated on real data.

Question: keep the split, drop it (count every assignment), or tune the threshold once real history exists?

C · Task families are a hard-coded mapping

Bug and Incident → defect; Story, New Feature, Task, Improvement, Change Request → delivery; Spike, Test, Risk → support; Epic → container; a Sub-task takes its parent's family; an unknown type name → delivery. The application has task types but no notion of "family". Feeds the three share columns.

Question: is this grouping right for your teams, and are there type names the mapping does not know?

D · "How a task was assigned" is a guess

Reporter equals assignee → self-picked; the task has a parent → project; otherwise manual. Nothing in the schema records this, so it is an inference from three columns, and the model gets one third each when a member had no task in the window. Feeds share_self_picked / manual / project_13w.

Question: does this match how people actually get work in WorkloadHub? If not, these three columns are noise and could go.

E · Remaining hours are recomputed, not read

The application stores remaining_estimate_hrs, but the feature matrix uses estimate − hours logged by the row's week for open_remaining_hrs and due_hrs_h. That is on purpose: a training row must see what was known at the time, and the stored field only has today's value. The facts shown to the leader, by contrast, use the stored field. The two can disagree.

Question: acceptable, or should the leader-facing pressure facts and the features use one definition?

F · Sentinel values stand in for "unknown"

52 weeks means "never received a task" and "no deadline in sight"; one third means "no tasks to measure a share on"; 1.0 means "no finished task to measure estimation bias on"; cycle_days_13w is left blank (NaN, which the booster handles as missing) when nothing finished. The first three are numbers the model cannot tell apart from a real 52 weeks, a real third or a real 1.0.

Question: leave as is, or make every "unknown" a blank so the booster treats it as missing?

G · Four fields are read as they stand today

The history replay rewinds assignments, statuses and logs to the row's week, but reopened_from_done, projects.status, due_date and original_estimate_hrs have no history and are read as of today. A row for last March therefore "knows" a due date that may have been set in June. The effect is small and known, and a task's current due date is the best available stand-in, but it is hindsight.

Question: acceptable? The alternative is to drop reopen_rate_13w and the project status columns.

H · A project deadline is derived from its tasks

Projects have no start or end date, so proj_first_due_weeks takes the nearest due date among the team's open tasks, per project, and keeps the minimum. It is the module's replacement for a missing field.

Question: will projects gain an end date in the application? If so this column should read it instead.

I · Capacity defaults to 44 h when the app has no row

When user_capacity has no row for a week, capacity is the member's latest base_capacity_hrs, or the module's 44 h default, over the week's working days, minus leave hours. The 44 h and the 8.8 h day are settings of the module, not of the application.

Question: does the application write a user_capacity row for every member and week, or only for some? The answer decides how often the leave table matters to the weekly features at all (section 6).

Recorded by the application but not used by the model

  • tasks.remaining_estimate_hrs is used by the leader-facing facts (open work, hours due) but not by any feature column (item E).
  • team_capacity (total and allocated hours per team-week) is shown to Copilot as the team's own plan and is not an input to the forecast.
  • The first design also planned planned_hrs_h, planned_remaining_h and team_planned_hrs_h from a planned_week on tasks. They were dropped when the forecast was retargeted at logged hours on 2026-09-13, and the column is not read.
  • task_comments, task_attachments, notifications, project_history, job_titles and user_roles are never read.

6 · What the switch from absences to personal leaves touches

The forecast reads absence in exactly three places, and none of them is a feature column by itself:

  1. absence_hrs_h and available_hrs_h, fallback path only. When user_capacity holds a row for the target week, both columns come from that row and the leave table is never consulted. Only for a week without a row does the module sum the member's absence hours over that week's working days. After the switch, that sum comes from expanding each APPROVED personal_leaves row into its working days, with absence_hours spread evenly over them and the times deciding a partial first or last day.
  2. The day placement (offDays). A day whose absence hours reach that day's gross capacity is a full leave day: no predicted hours land on it and the week's hours move to the other days. This is what makes a member's forecast honest around a vacation, and it is independent of user_capacity.
  3. The day capacity shown to the leader (dayCapacity). Without a capacity row, a day's capacity is the base over five days minus that day's leave hours.

Nothing in the model's own history columns (lag*, roll_*, arrivals, open work, team, identity) reads absences at all, so retraining behaviour does not change with the switch; only the availability of the target week and the placement on days do. PENDING leaves will be listed in the facts for the narrative to mention, the way pending holidays already are, and never reduce capacity.
